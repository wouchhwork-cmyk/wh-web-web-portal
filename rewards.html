How the programs are doing

Value is net of anything taken back — a clawed-back award stops counting, so this is what the programs have actually cost rather than what they ever granted. Points and each currency are shown separately and never added together.

Reward programs

A program says when it runs and what it can spend. Its rules say what a customer has to do, and what they get for it. A program is switched on separately from its dates — one scheduled for next month is active with a start date in the future.

New program

The timezone decides when “once a day” resets and when a happy hour opens, so set it to where the business actually is.

Rules in

Rules are evaluated in priority order and several can fire on one event — a base rule and a bonus rule both granting is intended, and each keeps its own cap.

What they get

A rule can award several things at once — 20 points and a free coffee. They are applied together or not at all, so a customer is never told they earned both and given one.

When it applies

Leave this empty and the rule applies whenever the trigger happens. Add a window and it only applies inside those hours, in the program’s own timezone.

Days
to

A window ending before it starts runs past midnight — 22:00 to 02:00 is Friday night and the first two hours of Saturday. Add several for one day if there is a lunch window and a dinner window.

A customer’s balance

Points and each currency are separate balances and are never added together. Awards that granted nothing are listed too — that is what answers “why didn’t I get my points?”

Adjust a balance

A manual credit or debit for one customer. It is recorded against you by name, in the audit log and on the ledger entry, and a reason is required.
